4:T379a,<p>Every token in a sequence looks at every other token, decides which ones matter to it, and rebuilds itself as a blend of what it found.</p>
<p>The <em>self</em> is the important word. The things being looked at are the other tokens in the same sequence — not a separate input, not a stored document. A sentence is examined against itself.</p>
<h2 id="why-that-is-necessary"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#why-that-is-necessary">Why that is necessary</a></h2>
<p>A word's embedding is fixed. <code>charge</code> gets the same vector every time, whatever the sentence, because the lookup table does not know what sentence it is in.</p>
<p>But <code>charge</code> in a courtroom and <code>charge</code> in a battery are different words wearing the same spelling. Something has to make the representation depend on the surroundings, and self attention is that something.</p>
<h2 id="three-roles-per-token"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#three-roles-per-token">Three roles per token</a></h2>
<p>Each token produces three vectors, all from its own embedding through three learned matrices:</p>
<p><strong>Query</strong> — what this token is looking for. <strong>Key</strong> — what this token offers to anyone looking. <strong>Value</strong> — what it actually contributes if chosen.</p>
<p>A token's query is compared against every token's key, including its own. The comparison is a dot product, divided by the square root of the vector width to keep the numbers in a sane range, and passed through softmax so the results are weights that sum to 1. The output is those weights applied to the values.</p>

<p>All three come from the same sequence. That single fact is what makes it self attention.</p>
<h2 id="the-same-word-twice"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#the-same-word-twice">The same word, twice</a></h2>
<p>Take four dimensions and pretend they mean something: legal, electrical, generic, physical. Give <code>charge</code> an ambiguous embedding, sitting equally in the legal and electrical directions:</p>
<p><code>charge = [0.5, 0.5, 0.6, 0.2]</code></p>
<p>Now run it in two sentences.</p>

<p>The legal and electrical dimensions swap almost exactly. The cosine similarity between the two outputs is <strong>0.59</strong>, from an input that was byte-identical in both cases.</p>
<p>That is the whole mechanism. The token did not become legal or electrical by itself — it absorbed a share of its neighbours' values, and the neighbours were different.</p>
<p>Notice too that the attention weights were nearly flat, around a third each. Attention did not need to pick a winner. The output changed because <em>what was there to average over</em> changed, which is a quieter and more common way for it to work than the dramatic one-token-attends-strongly-to-another picture suggests.</p>
<h2 id="why-it-replaced-what-came-before"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#why-it-replaced-what-came-before">Why it replaced what came before</a></h2>
<p><strong>Everything happens at once.</strong> Previous sequence models read left to right, each step waiting on the last. Self attention computes all positions in parallel, which is what makes training on large data practical.</p>
<p><strong>Distance stops mattering.</strong> A token at position 400 reaches a token at position 3 directly, in one operation. In a recurrent model that information had to survive 397 sequential updates, and it usually did not.</p>
<p><strong>Words stop having one meaning.</strong> As above.</p>
<h2 id="several-at-once"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#several-at-once">Several at once</a></h2>
<p>One set of Q, K and V matrices learns one way of relating tokens. Running several in parallel, each with its own matrices, lets different heads pick up different relationships — one tracking grammatical agreement, another nearby modifiers, another something with no clean name.</p>
<p>Each head produces its own output, the outputs are concatenated, and a final matrix mixes them back to the expected width.</p>
<h2 id="the-one-variant-that-matters"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#the-one-variant-that-matters">The one variant that matters</a></h2>
<p>In a model that generates text, a token must not see what comes after it — during training the whole sentence is present, so without intervention position 3 could read position 5 while learning to predict position 4.</p>
<p>The fix is to set those scores to negative infinity before the softmax, so their weights come out as exactly zero. Same mechanism, with the upper triangle switched off.</p>
<p>Encoders skip the mask and let every token see the whole sequence, because there is nothing to predict in order.</p>
<h2 id="the-short-version"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#the-short-version">The short version</a></h2>
<ul>
<li>Every token attends to every token in the same sequence, itself included.</li>
<li><em>Self</em> means queries, keys and values all come from that one sequence.</li>
<li>Each token compares its query against all keys, softmaxes the scores, and blends the values.</li>
<li>Because the blend is over its neighbours, a fixed embedding produces different output in different sentences — 0.76 legal in one, 0.76 electrical in the other.</li>
<li>Weights are often fairly flat; what changes is what there is to average.</li>
<li>All positions compute in parallel, and distance costs nothing.</li>
<li>Multiple heads learn different relationships and are concatenated.</li>
<li>Masking future positions turns it into the version generation needs.</li>
