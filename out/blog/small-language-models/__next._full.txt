11:T2373,<p>A small language model is the same thing as a large one, with fewer parameters. Usually under about ten billion, often between half a billion and nine.</p>
<p>That number is not the interesting part. What matters is what it implies: a model of that size fits in memory you already own, which changes where it can run, what it costs, and how fast it answers.</p>
<h2 id="parameters-are-not-the-unit-that-matters"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#parameters-are-not-the-unit-that-matters">Parameters are not the unit that matters</a></h2>
<p>A parameter count is not a size. Bytes are a size, and how many bytes a parameter takes depends on the precision it is stored in.</p>









































<table><thead><tr><th>params</th><th>fp16</th><th>int8</th><th>int4</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>0.5B</td><td>1.00 GB</td><td>0.50 GB</td><td>0.25 GB</td></tr><tr><td>1B</td><td>2.00 GB</td><td>1.00 GB</td><td>0.50 GB</td></tr><tr><td>3B</td><td>6.00 GB</td><td>3.00 GB</td><td>1.50 GB</td></tr><tr><td>8B</td><td>16 GB</td><td>8.00 GB</td><td>4.00 GB</td></tr><tr><td>70B</td><td>140 GB</td><td>70 GB</td><td>35 GB</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Quantisation — storing weights in fewer bits — is what turns a parameter count into something that fits. A 3B model at full precision needs 6 GB and will not run on a phone. The same model at four bits needs 1.5 GB and will.</p>

<p>A phone runs up to about 3B. A laptop or a single consumer GPU runs up to about 8B. A 70B model needs a datacentre card even at four bits.</p>
<h2 id="why-they-are-fast"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#why-they-are-fast">Why they are fast</a></h2>
<p>Generating a token means reading every weight out of memory. So speed is set by memory bandwidth divided by model size, and that estimate is usually close:</p>





































<table><thead><tr><th></th><th>0.5B</th><th>1B</th><th>3B</th><th>8B</th><th>70B</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>phone, ~50 GB/s</td><td>200</td><td>100</td><td>33</td><td>13</td><td>1</td></tr><tr><td>laptop, ~100 GB/s</td><td>400</td><td>200</td><td>67</td><td>25</td><td>3</td></tr><tr><td>one GPU, ~900 GB/s</td><td>>1000</td><td>>1000</td><td>600</td><td>225</td><td>26</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Tokens per second, four-bit weights.</p>
<p>A 3B model at four bits is 1.5 GB, and a phone with 50 GB/s of bandwidth can read that about 33 times a second — which is roughly the generation speed people actually measure on phones. The estimate is crude and it is not far off, because reading the weights really is the dominant cost.</p>
<p>The same arithmetic explains the latency difference. A small model starts answering in tens of milliseconds because there is little to read; a large one takes seconds.</p>
<h2 id="why-small-models-got-good"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#why-small-models-got-good">Why small models got good</a></h2>
<p>A 3B model today is not the same thing as a 3B model from a few years ago, and three changes account for most of that.</p>
<p><strong>Better data.</strong> Training on a filtered, high-quality corpus rather than as much scraped text as possible. With few parameters, what they are spent on matters more.</p>
<p><strong>Distillation.</strong> Train the small model to reproduce a large model's output distribution rather than just to predict the next token from raw text. The larger model's judgement gets compressed into the smaller one.</p>
<p><strong>Architectural savings.</strong> Techniques that improve efficiency without adding parameters — sharing keys and values across attention heads, encoding position by rotation rather than a learned table. Every parameter not spent on overhead is available for capability.</p>
<h2 id="what-is-actually-lost"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#what-is-actually-lost">What is actually lost</a></h2>
<p>The trade is real, and it is worth being precise about which parts get worse.</p>
<p><strong>Stored facts.</strong> Parameters are where knowledge lives, and there are far fewer of them. A small model is thinner on anything obscure, and it does not signal the difference between recall and invention.</p>
<p><strong>Multi-step reasoning.</strong> Long chains of arithmetic or logic degrade first. It will often produce a confident, well-formed, wrong answer.</p>
<p><strong>Using long context.</strong> Supporting 128K tokens and <em>using</em> 128K tokens are different claims. Small models usually support long windows and get worse at finding things buried in them.</p>
<p><strong>Prompt sensitivity.</strong> Rephrasing a prompt shifts the answer more than it would with a large model. This is the failure that surprises people in production, because it looks like randomness.</p>
<h2 id="what-they-are-good-at"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#what-they-are-good-at">What they are good at</a></h2>
<p>Narrow, well-defined work — classification, extraction, formatting, deciding which tool to call. Tasks with a small output space and a clear right answer, where breadth of world knowledge is irrelevant.</p>
<p>High volume, where the price difference stops being abstract. And anything with a latency budget under a couple of hundred milliseconds.</p>
<p>And anything that cannot leave the device. A model running locally sends nothing anywhere, which for medical, financial, or confidential material is not an optimisation but the requirement.</p>
<p>Fine-tuning is also within reach: adapting a 3B model on a single consumer GPU is a matter of hours, and a 3B model fine-tuned on ten thousand examples of your specific task will usually beat a much larger general model at that task.</p>
<h2 id="the-short-version"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#the-short-version">The short version</a></h2>
<ul>
<li>Small means under roughly ten billion parameters.</li>
<li>Bytes, not parameters, decide where a model runs — and quantisation is what converts one into the other.</li>
<li>At four bits: 3B fits a phone, 8B fits a laptop, 70B still needs a datacentre GPU.</li>
<li>Speed is memory bandwidth divided by model size, and that estimate lands close to reality.</li>
<li>Better data, distillation from larger models, and cheaper architecture are why small models improved.</li>
<li>What degrades: stored facts, multi-step reasoning, long-context use, and stability under rephrasing.</li>
<li>Best for narrow tasks, high volume, tight latency, and anything that must stay on the device.</li>
