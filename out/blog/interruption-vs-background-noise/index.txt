12:T1db0,<p>Barge-in lets a user interrupt a voice agent mid-sentence. Implemented naively — stop speaking whenever the microphone picks up sound — it makes the agent unusable anywhere but a silent room. It stops when the user coughs, when a car passes, when someone in the background talks, when the user says "mm-hmm" to show they are following.</p>
<p>The task is a classification problem with a deadline: while the agent is speaking, decide whether each sound from the user's side is a genuine attempt to take the turn.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-microphone-hears"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#what-the-microphone-hears">What the microphone hears</a></h2>
<p>During the agent's speech, the incoming audio contains:</p>





























<table><thead><tr><th>sound</th><th>should the agent stop?</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>"Wait — no, the Tuesday one"</td><td><strong>yes</strong> — a real interruption</td></tr><tr><td>"Mm-hmm", "yeah", "right"</td><td>no — a backchannel; keep going</td></tr><tr><td>cough, sneeze, laugh, sigh</td><td>no</td></tr><tr><td>TV, traffic, other people talking</td><td>no</td></tr><tr><td>the agent's own voice echoing back</td><td>no — and the most dangerous</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Only the first should stop the agent. It is also the only one that typically keeps going.</p>
<h2 id="duration-the-first-crude-signal"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#duration-the-first-crude-signal">Duration: the first, crude signal</a></h2>
<p>Most non-interruptions are short. A backchannel is a word or two; a cough is a burst. A real interruption is a phrase or a sentence. So the simplest discriminator is to wait: require a certain amount of continuous speech before stopping.</p>
<p>Model backchannel durations as log-normal around a median of 350 ms, and coughs around 250 ms. The share of each that would still trigger a stop after waiting <em>t</em> ms:</p>



































<table><thead><tr><th>wait before stopping</th><th>backchannels that trigger</th><th>coughs that trigger</th><th>delay on real interruptions</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>150 ms</td><td><strong>98.3%</strong></td><td>84.7%</td><td>150 ms</td></tr><tr><td>300 ms</td><td>65.0%</td><td>35.8%</td><td>300 ms</td></tr><tr><td>500 ms</td><td>18.6%</td><td>8.3%</td><td>500 ms</td></tr><tr><td>700 ms</td><td><strong>4.2%</strong></td><td>2.0%</td><td><strong>700 ms</strong></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>The trade is plain. Responding quickly to real interruptions means stopping for nearly every "mm-hmm". Ignoring backchannels reliably means making the user talk over the agent for most of a second before it yields — which feels like not being listened to.</p>

<h2 id="better-signals-than-duration"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#better-signals-than-duration">Better signals than duration</a></h2>
<p>Duration is a starting point. Systems that get this right combine several signals:</p>
<p><strong>Is it speech at all?</strong> A voice activity detector trained to separate speech from non-speech rejects most coughs, laughter, music and traffic regardless of duration. Model-based VADs do this far better than energy thresholds.</p>
<p><strong>Is it the user's voice?</strong> Background conversations and TVs are speech but not the user's. Near-field versus far-field cues — loudness, reverberation — help, and some systems use the caller's voice characteristics from earlier in the call.</p>
<p><strong>Is it the agent's own voice?</strong> Acoustic echo cancellation removes the agent's playback from the microphone signal. Residual echo is a major source of false barge-ins on speakerphones; raising detection thresholds while the agent speaks is a common backstop.</p>
<p><strong>What are the words?</strong> A streaming transcript of the first few hundred milliseconds is often decisive. "Mm-hmm", "yeah", "okay", "right" are backchannels. "Wait", "stop", "no", "hang on", or a question word are interruptions. A small classifier on the partial transcript separates them far faster than waiting for duration to settle it.</p>
<p><strong>Where is the agent?</strong> Users interrupt more at natural points — the end of a list item, after a question. Context changes how likely each interpretation is.</p>
<h2 id="a-practical-policy"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#a-practical-policy">A practical policy</a></h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Duck, don't stop, at first.</strong> When possible user speech begins, lower the agent's volume immediately. It signals attention without committing.</li>
<li><strong>Classify quickly</strong> on the first 200–300 ms: is it speech, the user's voice, a backchannel phrase or an interrupting phrase?</li>
<li><strong>Stop</strong> on a confident interruption — or on any continued speech past a longer limit.</li>
<li><strong>Resume</strong> at full volume if it was a backchannel or noise, without repeating anything.</li>
<li><strong>Log every decision</strong> with audio, so false stops and missed interruptions can be reviewed and the thresholds tuned.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="what-to-take-away"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#what-to-take-away">What to take away</a></h2>
<p>While a voice agent speaks, most sounds from the user's side are not interruptions: backchannels, coughs, background speech and echo. Waiting for sustained sound filters them but delays real interruptions — at 150 ms the agent stops for 98.3% of backchannels, at 700 ms only 4.2%, but every genuine interruption then waits 700 ms.</p>
