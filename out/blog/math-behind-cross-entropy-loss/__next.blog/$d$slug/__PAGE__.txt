4:T9bf3,<p>A classifier does not output an answer. It outputs a probability for every possible answer.</p>
<p>Cross-entropy is how those probabilities get scored. It looks at the probability the model gave to the option that was actually correct, and turns it into a single number — small when that probability was high, large when it was low.</p>

<h2 id="why-the-log"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#why-the-log">Why the log</a></h2>
<p>The log is what makes the penalty non-linear.</p>

























<table><thead><tr><th>probability on the right answer</th><th><span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>−</mo><mi>log</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>p</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">-\log(p)</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1em;vertical-align:-0.25em;"></span><span class="mord">−</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.1667em;"></span><span class="mop">lo<span style="margin-right:0.0139em;">g</span></span><span class="mopen">(</span><span class="mord mathnormal">p</span><span class="mclose">)</span></span></span></span></th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>0.95</td><td>0.05</td></tr><tr><td>0.5</td><td>0.69</td></tr><tr><td>0.05</td><td>3.00</td></tr><tr><td>0.005</td><td>5.30</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Notice the shape. Between 0.95 and 0.5 the loss rises by 0.64. Between 0.05 and 0.005 — the same tenfold drop in probability — it rises by 2.30 again, and it keeps rising without limit as the probability approaches zero.</p>
<p>A model that is merely unsure gets a mild penalty. A model that is confident and wrong gets an enormous one. That asymmetry is deliberate: it is far worse to rule out the truth than to hesitate about it.</p>
<p>The minus sign is bookkeeping. Probabilities are at most 1, so their logs are at most 0. Negating makes the loss positive, with 0 as perfection.</p>
<h2 id="working-one-through"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#working-one-through">Working one through</a></h2>
<p>A model sorts a support ticket into one of four buckets. The ticket is really a <strong>bug</strong> report.</p>
<p>The model gives raw scores — logits — and softmax turns them into probabilities. Start with a model that is leaning the right way but not certain, with logits <code>[1.2, 2.8, 0.4, -0.6]</code>:</p>

























<table><thead><tr><th>bucket</th><th>probability</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>billing</td><td>0.1523</td></tr><tr><td><strong>bug</strong></td><td><strong>0.7542</strong></td></tr><tr><td>feature</td><td>0.0684</td></tr><tr><td>spam</td><td>0.0252</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Those sum to 1.0000. The loss is <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>−</mo><mi>log</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mn>0.7542</mn><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo>=</mo><mn>0.2822</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">-\log(0.7542) = 0.2822</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1em;vertical-align:-0.25em;"></span><span class="mord">−</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.1667em;"></span><span class="mop">lo<span style="margin-right:0.0139em;">g</span></span><span class="mopen">(</span><span class="mord">0.7542</span><span class="mclose">)</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">=</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">0.2822</span></span></span></span>.</p>
<p>Now sharpen the same model's opinion, so it puts 0.9696 on <code>bug</code>. The loss falls to 0.0309.</p>
<p>And now break it, so it puts 0.9608 on <code>billing</code> and only 0.0194 on <code>bug</code>. The loss jumps to 3.9399.</p>

<h2 id="the-two-class-version"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#the-two-class-version">The two-class version</a></h2>
<p>With only two options, one probability determines the other — if the chance of yes is <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>p</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">p</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">p</span></span></span></span>, the chance of no is <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>1</mn><mo>−</mo><mi>p</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">1 - p</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.7278em;vertical-align:-0.0833em;"></span><span class="mord">1</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2222em;"></span><span class="mbin">−</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2222em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">p</span></span></span></span>. So the formula is usually written as:</p>
<span class="katex-display"><span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="block"><semantics><mrow><mtext>BCE</mtext><mo>=</mo><mo>−</mo><mrow><mo fence="true">[</mo><mtext> </mtext><mi>y</mi><mi>log</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>p</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo>+</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mn>1</mn><mo>−</mo><mi>y</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mi>log</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mn>1</mn><mo>−</mo><mi>p</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mtext> </mtext><mo fence="true">]</mo></mrow></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\text{BCE} = -\left[\, y \log(p) + (1 - When the answer is yes, <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>y</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>1</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">y = 1</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0359em;">y</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">=</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">1</span></span></span></span> kills the second half and the loss is <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>−</mo><mi>log</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>p</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">-\log(p)</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1em;vertical-align:-0.25em;"></span><span class="mord">−</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.1667em;"></span><span class="mop">lo<span style="margin-right:0.0139em;">g</span></span><span class="mopen">(</span><span class="mord mathnormal">p</span><span class="mclose">)</span></span></span></span>. <p>With the true answer being yes: a prediction of 0.85 costs 0.1625, and a prediction of 0.30 costs 1.2040.</p>
<h2 id="in-a-language-model"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#in-a-language-model">In a language model</a></h2>
<p>A language model is a classifier whose options are every token in its vocabulary — often more than 50,000 of them.</p>
<p>The scoring is unchanged. At each position, find the probability it gave to the token that actually came next, take the negative log, and average over the sequence:</p>

<p>The correct class gets a negative gradient, so its logit is pushed up. Every wrong class gets a positive one, so their logits are pushed down, and by exactly the amount of belief each was wrongly given. They sum to zero, which is what keeps the probabilities summing to 1.</p>
<p>There is no division, no chain of intermediate terms, nothing that can blow up. That is why frameworks fuse softmax and cross-entropy into one operation instead of computing them separately.</p>
<h2 id="the-short-version"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#the-short-version">The short version</a></h2>
<ul>
<li>Cross-entropy scores a set of predicted probabilities against the answer that was actually right.</li>
<li>The zeros in the one-hot label collapse the sum to <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>−</mo><mi>log</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi>p</mi><mtext>correct</mtext></msub><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">-\log(p_{\text{correct}})</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1em;vertical-align:-0.25em;"></span><span class="mord">−</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.1667em;"></span><span class="mop">lo<span style="margin-right:0.0139em;">g</span></span><span class="mopen">(</span><span class="mord"><span class="mord mathnormal">p</span><span class="msupsub"><span class="vlist-t vlist-t2"><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.2806em;"><span style="top:-2.55em;margin-left:0em;margin-right:0.05em;"><span class="pstrut" style="height:2.7em;"></span><span class="sizing reset-size6 size3 mtight"><span class="mord mtight"><span class="mord text mtight"><span class="mord mtight">correct</span></span></span></span></span></span><span class="vlist-s">​</span></span><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.15em;"><span></span></span></span></span></span></span><span class="mclose">)</span></span></span></span> — only belief in the truth counts.</li>
<li>The log makes the penalty grow without limit as that belief approaches zero, so confident mistakes are punished hardest.</li>
<li>The two-class version is the same formula with one of its two halves always switched off.</li>
<li>Language models use it per token and report <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>e</mi><mi>L</mi></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">e^{L}</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.8413em;"></span><span class="mord"><span class="mord mathnormal">e</span><span class="msupsub"><span class="vlist-t"><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.8413em;"><span style="top:-3.063em;margin-right:0.05em;"><span class="pstrut" style="height:2.7em;"></span><span class="sizing reset-size6 size3 mtight"><span class="mord mtight"><span class="mord mathnormal mtight">L</span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span> as perplexity.</li>
<li>Paired with softmax, its gradient is just predicted minus actual, which is why the two are computed together.</li>
