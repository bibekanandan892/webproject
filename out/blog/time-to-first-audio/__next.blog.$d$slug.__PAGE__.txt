5:T1e58,<p>For a chat interface, the usual latency number is how long the whole answer takes. For a voice agent, that number barely matters. What the user experiences is the silence between finishing their sentence and hearing the agent start — <strong>time to first audio</strong>, or TTFA.</p>
<p>Once the agent is speaking, the user is listening, and listening takes far longer than any of the computation behind it.</p>
<h2 id="speech-is-slow-computers-are-fast"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#speech-is-slow-computers-are-fast">Speech is slow; computers are fast</a></h2>
<p>A 60-word reply is a normal conversational answer — three or four sentences. Spoken at a natural 150 words per minute, it lasts:</p>
<p><code>60 ÷ 150 × 60 =</code> <strong>24 seconds</strong> of audio.</p>
<p>Now the work to produce it:</p>

























<table><thead><tr><th>step</th><th>time</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>LLM time to first token</td><td>0.25 s</td></tr><tr><td>LLM generating ~78 tokens at 50 tokens/s</td><td>1.56 s</td></tr><tr><td>TTS synthesising 24 s of audio at 10× real time</td><td>2.40 s</td></tr><tr><td><strong>everything, done one after another</strong></td><td><strong>4.21 s</strong></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>If the agent waits for all of that before playing anything, the user sits in 4.21 seconds of silence — long enough to say "hello?" — and then listens for 24 seconds. If the agent streams, it can start speaking once the <em>first sentence</em> is generated and synthesised, typically well under a second, and produce the rest while the first sentence plays.</p>
<p>The total work is identical. The experience is completely different. Time-to-first-audio is the number that decides it.</p>
<h2 id="why-streaming-is-safe-the-generator-is-far-ahead"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#why-streaming-is-safe-the-generator-is-far-ahead">Why streaming is safe: the generator is far ahead</a></h2>
<p>Streaming only works if the pipeline produces audio faster than it is played; otherwise playback would catch up and stutter.</p>
<p>Speech at 150 words a minute, at about 1.3 tokens per word, consumes <strong>3.25 tokens per second</strong>. An LLM generating 50 tokens a second is more than <strong>15×</strong> faster than the listener needs. TTS at 10× real time is likewise far ahead of playback.</p>
<p>So once the first chunk is playing, the rest of the reply arrives with plenty of margin. Every millisecond of the user's waiting is in getting that first chunk out.</p>

<h2 id="what-ttfa-is-made-of"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#what-ttfa-is-made-of">What TTFA is made of</a></h2>
<p>With full streaming, time to first audio is:</p>
<p><code>endpointing + speech-to-text finalisation + LLM first token + tokens to the first speakable chunk + TTS first audio + network</code></p>
<p>Each piece can be pushed down:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Endpointing:</strong> decide sooner that the user has finished, using a turn-detection model rather than a long silence timer.</li>
<li><strong>First token:</strong> short, cache-friendly prompts; a model sized to the task.</li>
<li><strong>First chunk:</strong> do not wait for a full sentence if a clause will do. "Sure, " or "Okay — so " is speakable after two tokens.</li>
<li><strong>TTS first audio:</strong> a streaming TTS that emits audio for the first words before it has the rest of the sentence.</li>
<li><strong>Network:</strong> keep the TTS and LLM close to each other and to the telephony or WebRTC edge.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="shaping-the-first-words"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#shaping-the-first-words">Shaping the first words</a></h2>
<p>Because the first chunk is what the user waits for, the content of the reply's opening matters for latency, not just style.</p>
<p>An answer that begins <em>"Your appointment on Tuesday the 14th at 3pm has been confirmed"</em> cannot start until the model has decided all of that. An answer that begins <em>"Done — "</em> or <em>"Let me check. "</em> can start almost immediately while the substance is still being generated. Many voice agents are prompted to open with a short acknowledgement for exactly this reason.</p>
<p>The same idea extends to slow tools: say <em>"One moment while I look that up"</em> the instant a tool call starts, so the silence the user hears is short even when the work is not.</p>
<h2 id="measuring-it-properly"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#measuring-it-properly">Measuring it properly</a></h2>
<p>TTFA should be measured from the end of the user's speech to the first audio sample <em>the user hears</em> — after network transit and client buffering — not from when the server sent the first audio packet. Server-side timestamps routinely under-report the real gap by a hundred milliseconds or more. And it should be tracked at a high percentile: a handful of four-second silences shapes a user's opinion far more than a good median.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-take-away"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#what-to-take-away">What to take away</a></h2>
<p>A voice reply takes much longer to listen to than to produce: 24 seconds of speech from about 4 seconds of computation. If the agent waits for all of it, the user waits 4.21 seconds in silence; if it streams, the wait is just the time to the first speakable chunk.</p>
<p>Since generation runs 15× faster than speech is consumed, everything after the first chunk is easy.
