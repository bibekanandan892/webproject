12:T22b3,<p>Chain-of-thought prompting is asking the model to write out its reasoning before the answer, rather than producing the answer directly.</p>
<p>It works, and the reason it works is more mechanical than "thinking helps".</p>
<h2 id="where-the-computation-happens"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#where-the-computation-happens">Where the computation happens</a></h2>
<p>A model produces one token per forward pass through its layers. For a 32-layer model, that is 32 layers of processing — no more, whatever the question.</p>
<p>There is no hidden workspace. Nothing persists between tokens except the tokens themselves. So if a problem needs four intermediate quantities and the model is asked for the final number immediately, all four have to be resolved inside a single pass.</p>
<p>Ask for the working instead and the picture changes completely:</p>

























<table><thead><tr><th>tokens of working</th><th>layer passes applied to the question</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1</td><td>32</td></tr><tr><td>20</td><td>640</td></tr><tr><td>60</td><td>1,920</td></tr><tr><td>200</td><td>6,400</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Sixty tokens of reasoning is <strong>60× more computation</strong> spent on the same question. And it is not merely more — each new token can attend to every intermediate result already written, so step three is computed with steps one and two available as input rather than having to rederive them.</p>
<p>The written steps are the scratchpad. That is the whole mechanism.</p>

<h2 id="what-it-looks-like"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#what-it-looks-like">What it looks like</a></h2>
<p>A problem with a threshold in it:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>A crate weighs 14 kg. Freight is ₹85 for the first 5 kg and ₹12 for each additional kilogram, then 18% tax is added. What is the total?</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Asked for the number alone, a common failure is to multiply all 14 kg at the per-kilogram rate — 14 × 12 = 168, then ₹198.24 — which is wrong because the first five kilograms are covered by the base charge. The error is a single missed condition, and there was nowhere to notice it.</p>
<p>With the steps written:</p>
<pre><code>weight above the base       14 − 5 = 9 kg
extra charge                9 × 12 = ₹108
subtotal                    85 + 108 = ₹193
tax                         193 × 0.18 = ₹34.74
total                       ₹227.74
</code></pre>
<p>The threshold is handled in step one, where it is visible, and every later step reads a number that is already on the page.</p>
<h2 id="two-ways-to-ask"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#two-ways-to-ask">Two ways to ask</a></h2>
<p><strong>Zero-shot</strong> is a single instruction — "work through it step by step before answering" — and costs nothing. It is the first thing to try.</p>
<p><strong>Few-shot</strong> shows two or three worked examples in the format you want. It costs prompt tokens on every request but buys control over the <em>shape</em> of the reasoning: which intermediate quantities appear, in what order, with what units. Where the steps need to be machine-readable, or where the model keeps skipping a check you care about, the examples are how you specify it.</p>
<h2 id="where-it-makes-things-worse"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#where-it-makes-things-worse">Where it makes things worse</a></h2>
<p><strong>Tasks with nothing to decompose.</strong> Classifying tone, judging whether text is on-topic, picking one of four labels — these are single judgements. Asking for reasoning gives the model room to construct an argument away from a correct first instinct, and accuracy falls.</p>
<p><strong>Long chains.</strong> Every step must be right for the conclusion to be right. At 96% per step, three steps is 88.5% and eight is 72.1% — and a wrong intermediate is worse than no intermediate, because it is now in the context and everything after conditions on it. The model will not revisit it; it will build on it.</p>
<p><strong>Explanations that are not the reasoning.</strong> If a model settles on an answer and then produces steps, the steps are a justification rather than a derivation. They will look sound and will not be what produced the answer, which makes them misleading precisely where you wanted transparency.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-take-away"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#what-to-take-away">What to take away</a></h2>
<p>Chain of thought is not a trick for making a model more careful. It is a way of giving it somewhere to put intermediate results, and more forward passes in which to compute them.</p>
<p>Which tells you where it belongs: problems with genuine intermediate quantities, few enough steps that they can all be right, and where the working is worth its latency.
