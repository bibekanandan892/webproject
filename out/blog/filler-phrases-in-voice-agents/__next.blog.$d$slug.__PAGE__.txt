5:T1b05,<p>People fill silence when they need time to think. "Hmm, let me see." "Good question." "One moment." Listeners hear these as natural, and they reset the clock: a pause after "let me check" feels like checking, not like a dropped call.</p>
<p>Voice agents borrow the trick deliberately. A filler phrase is short speech, played immediately, that covers work the agent has not finished yet — most often a tool call, sometimes a slow first token from the language model.</p>
<h2 id="what-a-filler-buys"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#what-a-filler-buys">What a filler buys</a></h2>
<p>The user's experience of latency is the silence they hear. A filler converts part of that silence into speech, and if the work finishes before the filler does, the silence disappears altogether.</p>
<p>Suppose the agent's lookups — checking an order, searching availability — take a median of 800 ms but have a long tail, with 5% taking 2.5 s or more. How often does a filler of a given length hide the whole lookup?</p>

























<table><thead><tr><th>filler length</th><th>example</th><th>lookups fully covered</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>0.6 s</td><td><em>"Okay."</em></td><td>33.9%</td></tr><tr><td>1.2 s</td><td><em>"Let me check that for you."</em></td><td><strong>72.2%</strong></td></tr><tr><td>2.0 s</td><td><em>"Sure, let me pull up your order details."</em></td><td><strong>90.8%</strong></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>A one-second phrase hides most lookups completely. The rest still leave a gap — but a shorter one, after speech that explains it.</p>
<p>Longer fillers cover more, at a cost: they delay the answer on the lookups that were fast. If a lookup finishes in 300 ms, a 2-second filler makes the user wait 1.7 s longer than necessary. The filler should be roughly as long as the work it covers — and ideally chosen according to what is being done.</p>

<h2 id="two-ways-to-produce-them"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#two-ways-to-produce-them">Two ways to produce them</a></h2>
<p><strong>Pre-recorded or pre-synthesised.</strong> A set of short clips, played instantly when a tool call starts. Zero synthesis delay, consistent quality — but they are fixed, and must be in the same voice as everything else or the change is obvious.</p>
<p><strong>Generated by the model.</strong> The language model is prompted to say something before calling a tool, and that sentence is streamed to TTS as usual. Contextual — <em>"Let me check Tuesday's availability"</em> — and therefore more natural and more informative. But it costs a model turn's worth of latency before it can start, so it helps most when the tool is slow.</p>
<p>Many agents combine them: an instant short clip ("Sure —") the moment the user stops, then a model-generated, specific sentence while the tool runs.</p>
<h2 id="making-them-sound-natural"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#making-them-sound-natural">Making them sound natural</a></h2>
<p><strong>Vary them.</strong> Hearing "Let me check that for you" five times in one call is grating. With a set of <em>n</em> interchangeable fillers chosen at random, the chance of the same one twice in a row is 1/<em>n</em>: 33% with three, 20% with five, 10% with ten. Avoid immediate repeats explicitly.</p>
<p><strong>Make them specific when you can.</strong> "Checking your order now" tells the user the agent understood; "One moment" does not.</p>
<p><strong>Do not lie.</strong> "I've found it!" before the result is known will be wrong sometimes. Fillers should describe what is happening, not what will happen.</p>
<p><strong>Have a plan for the tail.</strong> If the tool is still running when the filler ends, the agent needs a second, different phrase — "Still looking, this one's taking a moment" — after a further second or two. Silence of three seconds after a filler is worse than silence without one, because it follows a promise.</p>
<p><strong>Keep them interruptible.</strong> A user who says "never mind" during a filler should be able to stop it like any other speech.</p>
<h2 id="where-fillers-are-the-wrong-answer"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#where-fillers-are-the-wrong-answer">Where fillers are the wrong answer</a></h2>
<p>Fillers hide latency; they do not remove it. An agent that says "let me check" before every reply — including replies that need no checking — sounds evasive and wastes the user's time. Use them for genuinely slow operations, and spend engineering effort making the common path fast enough not to need one.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-take-away"><a class="heading-anchor" href="#what-to-take-away">What to take away</a></h2>
<p>A filler phrase plays speech while slow work finishes, turning silence into conversation. With lookups at a median of 800 ms and a 2.5 s tail, a 1.2 s filler hides 72.2% of them completely, a 2.0 s one 90.8%.</p>
