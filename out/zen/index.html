06The Desktop Companion

Zen for Windows

The same idea, on the machine you actually work at. Zen Desktop watches which window has your attention, tags it Learn, Productive, Fun, or Waste, and keeps a per-display timeline of the day — with a floating widget, a browser extension for site-level tracking, and an on-device AI that can do the tagging for you.

Four tracking states

Every moment of the day resolves to one of four states, built from the foreground window, your input (counted, never recorded), media playback, and the lock state.

Active

Recent keyboard or mouse input in the focused window. Counts as usage.

Likely active

No input, but strong evidence you're still there — reading, watching a video, in a meeting. Counts as usage, shown lighter on the timeline.

Idle

You're at the desktop, but nothing suggests you're paying attention. Doesn't count.

Inactive

Locked, asleep, display off, or logged off. Doesn't count.

Floating widget

An always-on-top card showing what's tracking right now and today's totals. Draggable, resizable to a compact mode, click-through when you don't want it in the way, and toggled anytime with Ctrl+Alt+Z. It never takes keyboard focus and records no time itself.

Browser extension

Adds site-level tracking for Chrome, Edge, and Brave — which tab has focus, whether media is playing — reported straight to the desktop app on your own machine, never to a server. It isn't in a web store yet, so the setup wizard walks you through loading it unpacked (developer mode) in one pass.

On-device AI tagging

A built-in Qwen2.5 1.5Bmodel (~1.1 GB, downloaded once) tags new apps and sites for you, entirely on your PC. Already running Ollama? Zen can use that instead. Both are optional — skip AI tagging and do it by hand if you'd rather.

One setup wizard, everything configured

The installer opens a short guided setup the first time Zen Desktop runs. By the end, tracking, tagging, the extension, and sync are all decided — nothing is left half-configured.

07One Day, Every Device

All Devices

Turn on sync and Zen merges your phone and your PC into a single day — one timeline, one set of totals — through a hidden folder in your own Google Drive. It's opt-in, and off by default.

Your Drive, not ours

Zen writes to a hidden, app-only folder in your Google Drive (Drive's appDataFolderscope) — the kind of folder you can't browse from the regular Drive app, and other apps can't see. Nothing goes to a server either of us runs.

1 phone
1 PC

One phone and one PC per Google account is the supported setup. Sign in on a second device and Zen asks you to replace a seat before it uploads anything — your existing device's data isn't touched until you choose.

When phone and PC overlap

If both devices show activity in the same minute, the stronger tracking state wins (Active beats Likely active). A tie falls back to your "prefer phone or desktop" setting — phone by default — and overlapping time is also marked separately as "both screens" so it's never silently dropped.

Off until you turn it on

Sync is disabled by default on both platforms. Nothing leaves the device until you sign in and switch it on in Settings.

Titles stay local unless you say otherwise

Categories, tags, and how long you spent sync by default — page and video titles only sync if you separately turn on "Sync titles." Private/incognito browsing always syncs as a generic "private" label.

Syncs on a schedule, not constantly

Each device checks in every 15–60 minutes (your choice), plus whenever you open the All Devices tab with stale data, or tap refresh yourself.

08Product Capabilities

Complete Feature Architecture

From unskippable app blockers and mindful breathing wait timers to on-device Gemma 3 1B AI classification, Zen is engineered for intentional living.

Focus & Wellbeing

Friction against impulse

Wait Timer

A short breathing countdown before an app opens. Breaks muscle-memory phone pickups and prevents subconscious mindless launches.

Unbypassable App Blocker

Lock out any app from 1 hour to 30 days. Auto-expires, strictly unskippable. Overlay window cannot be bypassed or closed.

Blocking Schedules

Block sets of apps automatically on chosen days and timeframes (supports overnight schedules e.g., 22:00 to 07:00).

Per-App Grayscale

Drains the color while a chosen app is open, eliminating high-contrast dopamine cues while leaving your phone normal elsewhere.

Quiet Notification Inbox

Intercepts distracting alerts and holds them safely in Zen's own local inbox. Review them on your schedule without status bar dings.

Screen Time Engine

True usage without inflation

Apps Tab

Productive-time switch, 7-day visual chart (Learn · Fun · Waste · rest of phone time; tap any day for numbers), and time per app today.

Websites Tab

Chrome tracking switch, time per site today grouped cleanly by domain, with its assigned Learn/Fun tag.

YouTube Tab

Dedicated switch; partitions today's watch time into Learn, Fun, Shorts, and untagged. Logs exact time per channel and per video.

Anti-Inflation Filter

Stuck screens (Recents view, incoming call screens, notification shades, system overlays) no longer artificially inflate active app time.

Media & Web Telemetry

Active watch duration only

Long Videos vs Idle

Only time actually playing media counts. Paused tabs or background idle do not inflate stats. Saves channel name and video title.

Shorts Isolation

Detected directly on screen via accessibility heuristics and isolated separately as Waste, distinct from long-form educational watch time.

Chrome Domain Tracking

Extracts top-level domains in real-time. Well-known domains (GitHub, Wikipedia, Arxiv) are pre-tagged from a built-in list.

User Tag Authority

Tap Learn or Fun on any channel or site. Your personal manual tag always wins over automated heuristics. Untagged items are surfaced first.

10Trust & Security

Privacy & Permissions: Each Strictly Optional

Zen has zero analytics SDKs, zero cloud tracking, zero remote servers, and zero telemetry — on Android or on Windows. Every capability is powered strictly by local APIs, and cross-device sync is opt-in and off by default.

On Windows, the same rules

  • Everything lives in a local SQLite database on the PC — nothing leaves it unless sync is turned on.
  • Keyboard and mouse input is counted to judge activity, but never recorded — no keylogging.
  • Browser URLs are stored as domains only by default; a setting can turn on full URLs.
  • All Devices sync writes to a hidden, app-only folder in your own Google Drive — never a third-party server.

100% Local SQLite DB

All usage metrics, schedules, favorites, and watch logs live solely inside an encrypted Room database on your physical device.

Narrow, Stated Reads

Zen Focus mostly sees only which app is open. It reads content in two places: Chrome's address bar (kept as the domain only) and YouTube's view IDs (to detect Shorts). It never reads what you type.

Excluded from Backups

Your notification inbox and sensitive usage logs are explicitly excluded from Google Drive and Android cloud transfers (data_extraction_rules.xml).

Permission Manifest & System Access Map

Every permission is requested just-in-time only when you enable the corresponding feature.

Usage Access

Optional

Granted via Android Settings → Usage Access

Why it's needed: Calculates total app screen time, today's timeline bars, and screen-free breaks.

Under the hood: Reads standard Android UsageStatsManager event intervals. Does not read content.

Accessibility (Zen Focus)

Optional

Granted via Android Settings → Accessibility

Why it's needed: Powers wait timers, unskippable app blocking, per-app grayscale, YouTube Shorts isolation, Chrome domain tracking, and unlock rewards.

Under the hood: Window-content access is on, but used in exactly two places: in Chrome it reads the address bar and keeps only the domain (never the full URL), and in YouTube it checks view IDs (never text) to spot the Shorts player. Everywhere else it only sees which app is in front — never what you type, passwords, or messages.

Notification Access

Optional

Granted via Android Settings → Notification Listener

Why it's needed: Tracks active YouTube video playback duration (via media session notifications) and routes distracting apps to Zen's quiet in-app inbox.

Under the hood: Filtered notifications are saved in a local Room database capped at the 200 newest items and completely excluded from cloud backups.

Write Secure Settings (ADB)

Optional · One-Time

Granted once via adb shell pm grant

Why it's needed: Enables per-app monochrome (system-level grayscale toggle).

Under the hood: Requires a single adb pm grant command. Completely optional if you don't use grayscale.

11Specifications & Transparency

Technical Specifications & Notes

Full technical clarity on what Zen includes, how it is built, and what to expect when sideloading.

Good to Know

Distribution via Direct APK

Not distributed on Google Play Store due to high-privilege focus permissions (Accessibility, Draw-Over-Apps, Notification Access). Download and install directly via GitHub Releases or build from source.

Package Size Breakdown

Full APK is ~27 MB, largely composed of Google's native MediaPipe GenAI on-device inference runtime. A ~2 MB ultra-lite build without on-device AI is also supported.

AI Model Download (555 MB)

The Gemma 3 1B LLM model weights are not bundled in the APK to keep installs nimble. Download the .task file once from Hugging Face for free and select it in Zen settings.

Browser Scope: Chrome

Website domain telemetry and tab tracking currently support Google Chrome. Support for Firefox and Brave is planned.

Metrics Retention Window

Per-app, per-site, and per-video breakdowns display granular logs for today. The 7-day overview chart summarizes rolling week trends across Learn, Fun, and Waste.

Video Titles Tracking

Video title logging is active from v0.1.0 onwards. Previous watch history displays aggregated channel-level duration.

This is a personal project, in beta, provided as is. It isn't distributed through the Play Store or the Microsoft Store, and neither installer is code-signed — see the notes below for what that means during install.

Installing on Android

  1. 1Download Zen.apk and open it. If prompted, allow your browser to install unknown apps.
  2. 2Google Play Protect may warn about an unknown developer — expected for any app installed outside the Play Store. Choose Install anyway.
  3. 3Open Zen and grant the permissions you want from Settings. On Android 13 and up, Accessibility and Usage access can appear greyed out as a "restricted setting" — tap Zen in Accessibility once and dismiss the popup, then go to App info → ⋮ → Allow restricted settings, then turn Accessibility (and Notification access) on.

Requires Android 8.0 (Oreo) or newer, 64-bit.

Installing on Windows

  1. 1Download and run ZenDesktopSetup.exe.
  2. 2Windows SmartScreen will likely show "Windows protected your PC"because the installer isn't code-signed. Click More info → Run anyway.
  3. 3The installer needs no admin rights. It opens a setup wizard on first run — it walks you through the browser extension (loaded unpacked, in developer mode) and everything else in one pass.

Requires Windows 10 (1809+) or Windows 11, 64-bit.

Uninstalling

Android: long-press the Zen icon (or app entry in the drawer) and choose Uninstall, same as any app — if Zen is your home screen, switch back to another launcher first in Settings → Apps → Default apps → Home app. Windows: Settings → Apps, find Zen Desktop, and uninstall — or use the "Uninstall Zen Desktop" shortcut in the Start menu. You'll be asked whether to also delete your local Zen data.

Both downloads come straight from GitHub Releases on the public zen-releases repository — this link always points at the newest build.
